# Can an AI read your page? Run the test yourself.

Three parts. Part 1 tests whether a model retains what you publish. Part 2 tests
whether anything finds you unprompted. They are different questions and most
advice conflates them.


## Part 2: The narrow-intent test

**What it answers:** does anything find you when you do not hand over the link?

Part 1 hands the model your URL. That is you doing the finding. This part does
not.

### The rule that makes it valid

**Never name your brand, your product or yourself in the prompt.** The moment you
do it is a vanity search and it proves nothing.

### Write four prompts describing only what your page uniquely publishes

1. **The most specific thing you have.** A named place, a named material, a
   number nobody else would use.
2. **The interpretation layer.** The thing a reseller structurally cannot say
   about your work.
3. **The policy differentiator.** How you do it, not what it is.
4. **Pure intent, no product spec.** How a real person would actually type it.

### Protocol, and hold it constant

Default consumer surface. Logged out where possible. No history. No follow-ups.
**Write down which surface and tier you used per prompt**, because a re-run in
three months is only comparable if you match them exactly.

### Read the result honestly

Getting nothing back on a question with exactly one correct answer in the world
does not mean you rank badly. It means nothing has read the page at all. Those
are different diagnoses with different fixes.

---

## What to expect

Part 1 is usually fixable in an afternoon. Part 2 usually is not. If Part 1 comes
back clean and Part 2 comes back empty, your page is legible and undiscovered,
and no amount of further page-editing changes that. Budget accordingly.
